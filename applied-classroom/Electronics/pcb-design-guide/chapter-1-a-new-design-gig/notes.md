# Chapter 1 — A New Design Gig

Read this before the quiz. The deck mixes two sources:

1. **The Hitchhiker’s Guide to PCB Design** — Chapter 1 *A New Design Gig* (PDF pages 8–11).
2. **SpaceX job posting** — `Electronics/spacex.txt` (*Sourcing Manager, Launch Vehicle Electronic Systems*). Use **Show in lecture** on those cards.

## What Chapter 1 is really about

Ian is hired as a PCB designer into a culture that treats design as “automated” and therefore easy. Old Bob’s process was never written down. Ian captures a schematic and layout with defaults, picks a cheap fab on star ratings, and brushes past **DFM** because he does not know what it means.

The lesson: graduation + automated tools ≠ a manufacturing-ready board. Process, mentoring, and supplier judgment matter.

## Checklist from the book (PDF p. 11)

When starting a PCB design role, ask:

- Is the culture compatible with how you work?
- Does the company treat the PCB role as valued, or as a button to press?
- Are the tools sufficient, and is there training?
- Will you have a design group or be a sole resource?
- Can you build a professional network and attend industry events?

## Why the SpaceX posting sits next to this chapter

The posting is not a layout job — it is **technical procurement** for launch-vehicle electronics (PCB, PCBA, components, instrumentation). The same literacy Ian lacked is what the role expects:

- Read drawings, fab notes, stack-ups, and BOMs
- Talk DFM and manufacturing complexity with engineering
- Build a supply base, run RFX, set SLAs/KPIs
- Dual-source, obsolescence, NPI → rate production
- Own the supplier, contract, and cost (planners own MRP)

Study Chapter 1 so you respect the design/fab process; study `spacex.txt` so you can speak the sourcing manager’s language in an interview.
