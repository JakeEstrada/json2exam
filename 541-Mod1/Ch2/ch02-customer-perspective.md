# Chapter 2 — Requirements from the Customer's Perspective

**Main idea:** Requirements are a partnership. Both sides have responsibilities, and the baseline is their shared agreement.

## Who counts as a "customer"

Not just the person using the software. A customer is anyone who:

- Pays for the product
- Requests the product
- Uses the product
- Benefits from the product
- Receives output from the product

Good software depends on good requirements, and good requirements depend on communication between customers and developers.

## Customer Bill of Rights

Customers have the right to expect analysts to:

- Speak in understandable language
- Learn the customer's business
- Properly document requirements
- Explain requirements documents
- Be respectful and professional
- Suggest alternatives
- Consider usability
- Discuss reuse opportunities
- Explain cost and schedule impacts of changes
- Deliver the agreed-upon system

## Customer Bill of Responsibilities

Customers must:

- Teach developers about their business
- Spend time clarifying requirements
- Be specific
- Make timely decisions
- Respect feasibility and cost estimates
- Prioritize requirements
- Review documents and prototypes
- Communicate changes quickly
- Follow the change process
- Respect the requirements engineering process

## Signoff and the baseline

Signoff does **not** mean "requirements can never change."

Signoff creates a **requirements baseline**: a snapshot of the agreed requirements at a point in time.

After the baseline:

- Changes are still allowed
- Changes need to be controlled
- Cost, schedule, and scope might need renegotiating

The baseline gives both sides confidence. The customer knows developers understand what to build. The developers know what they committed to deliver.
