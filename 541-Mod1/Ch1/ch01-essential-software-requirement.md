# Chapter 1 — The Essential Software Requirement

**Main idea:** Requirements answer "What are we actually supposed to build, and how do we make sure everyone agrees?"

## Why requirements matter

- Deciding *what* to build is one of the hardest parts of software development
- Bad requirements sink projects even when the code is good
- Fixing a requirements mistake gets more expensive the later you catch it

## Why requirements are hard

Lots of stakeholders, each with different expectations and assumptions:

- Customers
- Users
- Analysts
- Developers
- Testers
- Managers
- Legal staff
- Support staff

## What a requirement is

Something the system must do, a property it must possess, or a constraint it must follow.

## The four requirement levels

| Level | Answers |
|---|---|
| Business | Why the organization wants the product |
| User | What users need to accomplish |
| Functional | Specific behaviors the system must perform |
| Nonfunctional | Qualities and constraints |

Nonfunctional examples: performance, security, usability, availability, robustness.

## Requirements engineering breakdown

**Requirements engineering** splits into two parts:

1. **Requirements development**
2. **Requirements management** (controlling changes after agreement)

Requirements development has four activities:

- Elicitation
- Analysis
- Specification
- Validation

## Problems caused by bad requirements

- Rework
- Scope creep
- Ambiguity
- Overlooked users
- Inaccurate planning
- Gold plating

## Traits of a strong requirement

- Complete
- Correct
- Feasible
- Necessary
- Prioritized
- Unambiguous
- Verifiable
