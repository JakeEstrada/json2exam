# Chapter 4 — The Requirements (Business) Analyst

**Main idea:** The business analyst is the connection between customers/users and developers. Analyst is a role, not necessarily a job title.

## What the analyst does

The analyst leads the work of discovering, shaping, and controlling requirements:

- Gather requirements
- Study and analyze them
- Document them
- Validate them
- Manage them after agreement

That includes the business-level why (vision and scope), identifying stakeholders and users, facilitating disagreements, and running change control.

## Tasks in the engineering process

- **Elicitation:** interviews, workshops, observation, and other techniques to find out what people need
- **Analysis:** look for gaps, conflicts, derived requirements, and implied requirements
- **Specification:** write requirements clearly; models and prototypes help people react to something tangible
- **Validation:** confirm the documented requirements are the right ones; reviewing code and tests later checks that they were interpreted correctly
- **Prioritization:** stay neutral and help users and developers negotiate
- **Management:** plan, establish baselines, control changes, track status, keep the information organized

A **derived** requirement is inferred from other requirements. An **implied** requirement is needed even though nobody stated it directly.

## Skills

- Listening: stated needs, unstated needs, implied needs
- Interviewing and questioning: normal and unusual situations; uncertainties, disagreements, assumptions, ambiguities
- Analysis: abstraction and detail, wants vs needs, solutions vs requirements
- Facilitation: remain neutral, reduce tension, build trust
- Observation: notice what people do, not only what they say
- Writing and reading: express complex ideas clearly, use the customer's language
- Organization: turn a large, changing pile of information into a coherent whole
- Interpersonal skill: competing interests, mentoring, educating
- Creativity: useful capabilities the users have not thought of yet

## Knowledge

- Requirements engineering
- Software development
- Project management
- Risk management
- Quality engineering
- Application domain knowledge

## Who can fill the role

Software engineers, managers, domain experts, users, or anyone with the right knowledge and skills.

**Former user:** strong business and workplace knowledge. Risk: bias toward their own experience, too much focus on the UI, weaker technical background.

**Former developer:** strong technical skills. Risk: weaker social or business knowledge, jumping to implementation.

**Subject matter expert:** excellent domain understanding. Risk: writing their own requirements instead of the users', building something more ambitious than needed.
